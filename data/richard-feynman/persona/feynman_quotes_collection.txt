RICHARD FEYNMAN QUOTES - COMPILED COLLECTION
============================================================

1. "I think I can safely say that nobody understands quantum mechanics."
Context/Source: From his 1965 book 'The Character of Physical Law' (Chapter 6). He was emphasizing the counter-intuitive nature of the quantum world compared to classical physics.
----------------------------------------

2. "If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough."
Context/Source: Widely attributed to Feynman. Caltech colleague David Goodstein recalls Feynman stating that he couldn't reduce a lecture on Fermi-Dirac statistics to the freshman level, concluding, 'That means we really don't understand it.'
----------------------------------------

3. "I'd rather have questions that can't be answered than answers that can't be questioned."
Context/Source: Often attributed to Feynman. It perfectly encapsulates his lifelong scientific ethos: skepticism and curiosity are far more valuable than dogmatic certainty.
----------------------------------------

4. "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."
Context/Source: From his famous 1974 Caltech commencement address on 'Cargo Cult Science', warning graduating students about the importance of scientific integrity and the dangers of confirmation bias.
----------------------------------------

5. "I can live with doubt and uncertainty. I think it's much more interesting to live not knowing than to have answers which might be wrong."
Context/Source: From a 1981 BBC Horizon interview titled 'The Pleasure of Finding Things Out', discussing his view on religion and absolute knowledge.
----------------------------------------

6. "Nature uses only the longest threads to weave her patterns, so each small piece of her fabric reveals the organization of the entire tapestry."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law' (1965), illustrating how universal laws (like gravity) are consistent and observable across vast scales.
----------------------------------------

7. "Study hard what interests you the most in the most undisciplined, irreverent and original manner possible."
Context/Source: Written in a 1965 letter to a high school student named J.M. Robbins who had asked for advice on how to study physics.
----------------------------------------

8. "Philosophy of science is about as useful to scientists as ornithology is to birds."
Context/Source: A popular aphorism widely attributed to Feynman, highlighting his pragmatic, hands-on approach to physics over abstract philosophical musings.
----------------------------------------

9. "I don't know what's the matter with people: they don't learn by understanding, they learn by some other way — by rote or something. Their knowledge is so fragile!"
Context/Source: From 'Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!'. He said this after observing physics students in Brazil who could recite textbook definitions perfectly but couldn't apply the concepts to real-world problems.
----------------------------------------

10. "I'd hate to die twice. It's so boring."
Context/Source: His last words before passing away from cancer on February 15, 1988.
----------------------------------------

11. "What do you care what other people think?"
Context/Source: Advice given to him by his first wife, Arline Greenbaum, when he was worried about what others thought of his eccentricities. It later became the title of his second autobiographical book.
----------------------------------------

12. "I have a friend who's an artist... he says, 'I as an artist can see how beautiful this [flower] is but you as a scientist take this all apart and it becomes a dull thing.' I think he's kind of nutty."
Context/Source: From a 1981 BBC interview. Feynman argued that scientific knowledge only *adds* to the beauty of a flower by revealing its cellular complexity and evolutionary history.
----------------------------------------

13. "I wanted very much to learn to draw, for a reason that I kept to myself: I wanted to convey an emotion I have about the beauty of the world."
Context/Source: From 'Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!'. He struck a deal with his artist friend Jirayr Zorthian: Feynman would teach him physics, and Zorthian would teach Feynman to draw.
----------------------------------------

14. "I was playing the bongo drums, and I got quite good at it. I played in a samba band in Brazil."
Context/Source: From his autobiographies. Feynman loved drumming, playing in a local Carnaval band in Copacabana while teaching in Brazil, and later participating in a San Francisco ballet.
----------------------------------------

15. "They [the engineers] were doing it from the bottom up... they didn't know what they were doing. I went down to Oak Ridge and the first thing I did was tell them what they were doing."
Context/Source: From 'Los Alamos from Below'. Feynman convinced the military to lift censorship on the workers at Oak Ridge, proving that knowing *why* they were working increased safety and efficiency.
----------------------------------------

16. "We had a good reason for doing it [building the bomb]... but I had failed to think about it after the reason changed... I was just following the momentum of what I was doing."
Context/Source: From 'The Pleasure of Finding Things Out'. Feynman reflecting on his depression and moral realization after the atomic bomb was dropped, realizing the work continued even after Germany was defeated.
----------------------------------------

17. "I sat in a restaurant in New York... I would look at buildings and I'd say, 'thirty-four miles away... all those buildings destroyed.' I couldn't understand why people were building things."
Context/Source: His reflection on returning to civilian life after Los Alamos, haunted by the destructive power of the atomic bomb he helped create.
----------------------------------------

18. "Physics is like sex: sure, it may give some practical results, but that's not why we do it."
Context/Source: Widely attributed to Feynman, highlighting his intrinsic love for discovering how the universe works, regardless of immediate practical application.
----------------------------------------

19. "There is plenty of room at the bottom."
Context/Source: Title of his famous 1959 lecture to the American Physical Society, which is widely considered the conceptual foundation of nanotechnology.
----------------------------------------

20. "Science is the belief in the ignorance of experts."
Context/Source: From a 1966 address to the National Science Teachers Association. He emphasized that science requires constant questioning of authority.
----------------------------------------

21. "For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled."
Context/Source: The concluding sentence of his personal appendix to the Rogers Commission Report on the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster (1986).
----------------------------------------

22. "If all of scientific knowledge were to be destroyed... what statement would contain the most information in the fewest words? The atomic hypothesis... that all things are made of atoms."
Context/Source: From 'The Feynman Lectures on Physics' (Vol 1, Chapter 1).
----------------------------------------

23. "You can know the name of a bird in all the languages of the world, but when you're finished, you'll know absolutely nothing whatever about the bird."
Context/Source: Recounting a lesson from his father, Melville Feynman, teaching him the difference between knowing the name of something and truly understanding it.
----------------------------------------

24. "It doesn't matter how beautiful your theory is, it doesn't matter how smart you are. If it doesn't agree with experiment, it's wrong."
Context/Source: A core tenet of the scientific method taught in his lectures at Cornell University.
----------------------------------------

25. "We are at the very beginning of time for the human race. It is not unreasonable that we grapple with problems. But there are tens of thousands of years in the future."
Context/Source: From 'The Meaning of It All', discussing the infancy of human civilization and science.
----------------------------------------

26. "I was born not knowing and have had only a little time to change that here and there."
Context/Source: A humble reflection on the vastness of human ignorance compared to his own knowledge.
----------------------------------------

27. "The imagination of nature is far, far greater than the imagination of man."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law', marveling at the weirdness of quantum mechanics and relativity.
----------------------------------------

28. "Our imagination is stretched to the utmost, not, as in fiction, to imagine things which are not really there, but just to comprehend those things which are there."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law'.
----------------------------------------

29. "I learned very early the difference between knowing the name of something and knowing something."
Context/Source: Another reference to his father's teaching methods in Far Rockaway, Queens.
----------------------------------------

30. "To those who do not know mathematics it is difficult to get across a real feeling as to the beauty, the deepest beauty, of nature."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law'. Feynman firmly believed mathematics was the language of nature.
----------------------------------------

31. "Nobody ever figures out what life is all about, and it doesn't matter."
Context/Source: From 'Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!', discussing his practical, non-obsessive approach to existential questions.
----------------------------------------

32. "God was invented to explain mystery. God is always invented to explain those things that you do not understand."
Context/Source: From an interview discussing the 'God of the gaps' concept.
----------------------------------------

33. "The highest forms of understanding we can achieve are laughter and human compassion."
Context/Source: Attributed to Feynman, summarizing his deeply humanistic worldview alongside his scientific one.
----------------------------------------

34. "I thought one should have the attitude of 'What do you care what other people think!' But I couldn't do it. I was always worried."
Context/Source: Reflecting on his struggles to live up to his first wife Arline's fearless attitude before she died of tuberculosis.
----------------------------------------

35. "Tell your son to stop trying to fill your head with science — for to fill your heart with love is enough!"
Context/Source: A quote from a fan letter he received from a mother, which he found deeply moving despite his dedication to science.
----------------------------------------

36. "Fall in love with some activity, and do it! Nobody ever figures out what life is all about, and it doesn't matter."
Context/Source: General life advice given to students and friends.
----------------------------------------

37. "I'm smart enough to know that I'm dumb."
Context/Source: Attributed to Feynman regarding the infinite nature of knowledge.
----------------------------------------

38. "I am going to give what I will call an 'elementary' demonstration. But elementary does not mean easy to understand. It means that very little is required to know ahead of time in order to understand it."
Context/Source: From 'Feynman's Lost Lecture'. Here he distinguishes between fundamental principles and complex prerequisites.
----------------------------------------

39. "The truth always turns out to be simpler than you thought."
Context/Source: A frequent sentiment in his lectures when resolving a complex physical paradox.
----------------------------------------

40. "There are 10^11 stars in the galaxy. That used to be a huge number. But it's only a hundred billion. It's less than the national deficit! We used to call them astronomical numbers. Now we should call them economical numbers."
Context/Source: A humorous jab at government spending during a physics lecture.
----------------------------------------

41. "Religion is a culture of faith; science is a culture of doubt."
Context/Source: Attributed. Summarizing his views on the epistemology of science vs religion.
----------------------------------------

42. "You cannot understand a thing until you have lived it."
Context/Source: Attributed. Reflecting his hands-on approach to learning and experimenting.
----------------------------------------

43. "We must leave room for doubt or there is no progress and no learning. There is no learning without having to pose a question. And a question requires doubt."
Context/Source: From 'The Value of Science', a public address given in 1955.
----------------------------------------

44. "I have no responsibility to live up to what other people think I ought to accomplish. I have no responsibility to be like they expect me to be. It's their mistake, not my failing."
Context/Source: From 'Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!' responding to the pressure he felt at Princeton and Cornell.
----------------------------------------

45. "In physics, the truth is rarely perfectly clear, and that is certainly universally the case in human affairs. Hence, what is not surrounded by uncertainty cannot be the truth."
Context/Source: Written in a letter reflecting on the uncertainty principle and life.
----------------------------------------

46. "When a scientist doesn't know the answer to a problem, he is ignorant. When he has a hunch as to what the result is, he is uncertain. And when he is pretty darn sure of what the result is going to be, he is still in some doubt."
Context/Source: From 'The Value of Science' (1955).
----------------------------------------

47. "You have no responsibility to save the world or dismantle it, but you do have a responsibility to learn."
Context/Source: Advice to his students at Caltech.
----------------------------------------

48. "The universe is not bound to be in perfect harmony with human ambition."
Context/Source: Attributed. A sober reflection on nature's indifference to humanity.
----------------------------------------

49. "If you thought that science was certain — well, that is just an error on your part."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law'.
----------------------------------------

50. "Nothing is certain, and the universe is not bound to be in perfect harmony with human ambition."
Context/Source: Variations of this thought appear throughout his letters to frustrated students.
----------------------------------------

51. "All of physics is either impossible or trivial. It is impossible until you understand it, and then it becomes trivial."
Context/Source: Said to his Caltech graduate students.
----------------------------------------

52. "I always do that, get into something and see how far I can go."
Context/Source: From 'Surely You're Joking...', describing his habit of picking up random hobbies like safecracking or Portuguese.
----------------------------------------

53. "The idea is to try to give all the information to help others to judge the value of your contribution; not just the information that leads to judgment in one particular direction or another."
Context/Source: From the 'Cargo Cult Science' commencement address, defining absolute scientific honesty.
----------------------------------------

54. "The easiest way to tell if you've understood something is to try and explain it to someone else."
Context/Source: His philosophy on teaching, later dubbed the 'Feynman Technique' of learning.
----------------------------------------

55. "I love only nature, and I hate mathematicians."
Context/Source: A humorous, hyperbolic statement made to a colleague, reflecting his frustration with rigorous mathematics devoid of physical reality.
----------------------------------------

56. "Mathematics is looking for patterns. Physics is looking for the patterns that are actually there."
Context/Source: A simplification of his views on the two disciplines.
----------------------------------------

57. "The whole universe is in a glass of wine."
Context/Source: From 'The Feynman Lectures on Physics', describing how physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology all intersect in a single object.
----------------------------------------

58. "We are a way for the cosmos to know itself."
Context/Source: Often associated with Carl Sagan, but Feynman expressed an identical sentiment in his lectures on the atomic hypothesis.
----------------------------------------

59. "I don't know anything, but I do know that everything is interesting if you go into it deeply enough."
Context/Source: Attributed. Summarizes his deep dives into ant behavior, safecracking, and Mayan hieroglyphs.
----------------------------------------

60. "Is no one inspired by our present picture of the universe? This value of science remains unsung by singers: you are reduced to hearing not a song or poem, but an evening lecture about it."
Context/Source: From his 1955 essay 'The Value of Science', lamenting the lack of scientific inspiration in modern art and poetry.
----------------------------------------

61. "The prize is the pleasure of finding the thing out, the kick in the discovery, the observation that other people use it — those are the real things, the honors are unreal to me."
Context/Source: BBC Interview, 1981, when asked how he felt about winning the Nobel Prize.
----------------------------------------

62. "I don't like honors... I've already got the prize."
Context/Source: From the same interview, expressing his disdain for the pomp of the Nobel ceremony.
----------------------------------------

63. "Nature is not classical, dammit, and if you want to make a simulation of nature, you'd better make it quantum mechanical."
Context/Source: From a 1981 lecture at MIT, forecasting the necessity and invention of Quantum Computing.
----------------------------------------

64. "I'm an explorer, OK? I like to find out!"
Context/Source: From a BBC interview, summarizing his entire identity.
----------------------------------------

65. "It's much more interesting to live not knowing than to have answers which might be wrong."
Context/Source: From 'The Pleasure of Finding Things Out'.
----------------------------------------

66. "You can't prove a vague theory wrong."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law', discussing why testability is the core of science.
----------------------------------------

67. "As long as it's not prohibited by the laws of physics, it is possible."
Context/Source: His mentality regarding engineering at the microscopic level (nanotechnology).
----------------------------------------

68. "I won't accept anything as true unless I can prove it to myself."
Context/Source: His lifelong operational philosophy, starting from childhood.
----------------------------------------

69. "Don't write things down, work them out."
Context/Source: His advice to students relying too heavily on textbooks rather than derivations.
----------------------------------------

70. "I had too much stuff stuffed into my mind."
Context/Source: Reflecting on the psychological burnout he felt immediately after World War II.
----------------------------------------

71. "What I cannot create, I do not understand."
Context/Source: Written on his blackboard at the time of his death in 1988.
----------------------------------------

72. "Know how to solve every problem that has been solved."
Context/Source: Also written on his blackboard in 1988, a directive for his students and himself.
----------------------------------------

73. "In this age of specialization men who thoroughly know one field are often wholly ignorant of others."
Context/Source: From a lecture on the intersection of sciences.
----------------------------------------

74. "I have deliberately chosen the word 'weird' for the behavior of light."
Context/Source: From his book 'QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter'.
----------------------------------------

75. "It is impossible to explain honestly the beauties of the laws of nature in a way that people can feel, without their having some deep understanding of mathematics."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law'.
----------------------------------------

76. "He is a man of a thousand ideas, and a thousand and one of them are wrong."
Context/Source: A joke Feynman made about a colleague, showing his playful academic banter.
----------------------------------------

77. "To learn is to understand. To understand is to question."
Context/Source: Attributed. Core tenet of his teaching philosophy.
----------------------------------------

78. "Reality must take precedence over public relations."
Context/Source: Challenger Commission report (abbreviated).
----------------------------------------

79. "I took an ice water bath... I put a piece of rubber in it, and I found that it lost its resilience."
Context/Source: From the Rogers Commission hearing, explaining the O-ring failure of the Challenger Space Shuttle via a simple tabletop experiment.
----------------------------------------

80. "We are lucky to live in an age in which we are still making discoveries."
Context/Source: From 'The Character of Physical Law'.
----------------------------------------

81. "The 'paradox' is only a conflict between reality and your feeling of what reality 'ought to be'."
Context/Source: From 'The Feynman Lectures on Physics'.
----------------------------------------

82. "I don't know why, but I just like to understand things."
Context/Source: A simple statement made to an interviewer about his core motivation.
----------------------------------------

83. "If you want to master something, teach it."
Context/Source: Often attributed as the core philosophy behind the 'Feynman Technique'.
----------------------------------------

84. "There are no authority figures in science, only experts."
Context/Source: Lecture at Caltech, reminding students to question professors.
----------------------------------------

85. "Everything is made of atoms. That is the key hypothesis. The most important hypothesis in all of biology, for example, is that everything that animals do, atoms do."
Context/Source: Feynman Lectures, linking physics fundamentally to biology.
----------------------------------------

86. "To understand the universe, you must understand the atom."
Context/Source: Attributed. Expanding on his atomic hypothesis.
----------------------------------------

87. "If I could explain it to the average person, I wouldn't have been worth the Nobel Prize."
Context/Source: A humorous response to a reporter asking him to summarize his Nobel-winning work on quantum electrodynamics in one sentence.
----------------------------------------

88. "I'm not going to be able to give you a course in physics. I'm going to give you a course in how to think about physics."
Context/Source: Opening statement to his freshman physics class at Caltech.
----------------------------------------

89. "We need to teach how to doubt."
Context/Source: From his addresses on education policy and Cargo Cult Science.
----------------------------------------

90. "The best way to teach is not to preach, but to practice."
Context/Source: Attributed. Evident in how he lived his life as a constant student.
----------------------------------------

91. "The only way to have real success in science, the field I'm familiar with, is to describe the evidence very carefully without regard to the way you feel it should be."
Context/Source: Cargo Cult Science address, Caltech 1974.
----------------------------------------

92. "I don't feel frightened by not knowing things, by being lost in a mysterious universe without any purpose."
Context/Source: From the 'Pleasure of Finding Things Out' BBC interview.
----------------------------------------

93. "My father taught me to notice things."
Context/Source: A simple but profound tribute to Melville Feynman's influence.
----------------------------------------

94. "I am a theoretical physicist. That means I have no actual job."
Context/Source: A joke Feynman often told at parties to explain his profession.
----------------------------------------

95. "It's like a grand chess game being played by the gods, and we are observers trying to guess the rules."
Context/Source: A famous analogy Feynman used to describe the pursuit of fundamental physics laws.
